## The Plan
I aspire to broker the use of creative code to create a series of generative artworks based on each of the books that we’ve read in our book club as a means of potentially reuniting the group. I will be using the p5.js editor and following tutorials and examples on YouTube and the p5.js website. My objectives for the project would be to strengthen my creative coding abilities, particularly in areas that I struggled with in previous projects, and engage with my friends, which is an aspect of my life that I have admittedly neglected in recent months. 

I’ve selected four books including:
‘The Bell Jar’ 
‘I’m A Fan’
‘Our Wives Under The Sea’ &
‘Things Have Gotten Worse Since We Last Spoke’.
These books have different themes, and I would like to explore them to create abstract pieces that either pull directly from the stories or merely represent one of the significant aspects of the novel. 

Initially, I had planned to create a book club review blog that would allow each member to input their thoughts on the book of the month. However, this felt slightly inauthentic, as the book club has adopted a more casual approach to discussing novels and it could potentially take the fun out of our dynamic. Instead, I will record their reactions to my artworks inspired by each book and observe the natural conversation that takes place.

Here is a short synopsis of each book and my ideas for their p5.js sketch. 

‘The Bell Jar’ by Sylvia Plath is a semi-autobiographical novel exploring the mental health struggles of Esther Greenwood, a bright young woman who spirals into depression and is institutionalised. I’d like to pull inspiration from the front cover, which features light blue concentric circles. Additionally, I would like to somehow incorporate the well-known fig tree analogy, though I think it would be kind of tacky to outright make a sketch of a fig. 

For clarity, the fig tree analogy (page 73):
‘I saw my life branching out before me like the green fig tree in the story. From the tip of every branch, like a fat purple fig, a wonderful future beckoned and winked. One fig was a husband and a happy home and children, and another fig was a famous poet and another fig was a brilliant professor, and another fig was Ee Gee, the amazing editor, and another fig was Europe and Africa and South America, and another fig was Constantin and Socrates and Attila and a pack of other lovers with queer names and offbeat professions, and another fig was an Olympic lady crew champion, and beyond and above these figs were many more figs I couldn't quite make out. I saw myself sitting in the crotch of this fig tree, starving to death, just because I couldn't make up my mind which of the figs I would choose. I wanted each and every one of them, but choosing one meant losing all the rest, and, as I sat there, unable to decide, the figs began to wrinkle and go black, and, one by one, they plopped to the ground at my feet.’.
The book club was prompted by one of the stories in Eric LaRocca’s novel, ‘Things Have Gotten Worse Since We Last Spoke’. The story is based on two lonely young women in an internet chat room in the early 2000s who eventually succumb to their most horrific desires. It was disgusting and twisted, and I couldn’t stop reading it – so I made them read it too. I’ve struggled to think of concepts for this book as most of the imagery is quite disturbing and I’m unsure if I want to recreate it. I was thinking I could focus on the internet chat room, or something related. 

‘I’m A Fan’ by Sheena Patel explores a toxic and seemingly unequal romantic relationship, where the narrator, a fan of a man she wants to be with, is also entangled with a woman he is having an affair with, all while the narrator and the man she desires are in long-term relationships with other people. It’s messy and chaotic and I would like to emphasise that obsession in my sketch, perhaps with an interactive feature. 

‘Our Wives Under the Sea’ by Julia Armfield follows the perspectives of wives Miri and Leah, when Leah returns home after being stranded in a submarine for six months during an underwater expedition, and Miri has to deal with all the profound changes occurring within her wife (both mentally and physically). There are many themes that I could draw from for inspiration, but I would like to incorporate the use of water in the sketch somehow, as it is consistent within the book even when Leah returns home. 

Although I didn’t actually read ‘Paradise Rot’ by Jenny Hval with my book club, I wanted to include my slime mould sketch which reminded me of the book (without the suspicious code).
